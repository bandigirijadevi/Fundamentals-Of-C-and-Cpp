# Starting CS College in India — First Day, Week & Months Guide

## First Day

**Practical**
- Carry all original documents + photocopies (10th/12th marksheets, admission letter, ID proof, passport photos) even if "everything was submitted online" — Indian colleges often ask for physical copies again.
- Note down: HOD's office, class coordinator, exam cell, and where notices are put up (physical board or a WhatsApp/Telegram group — ask seniors immediately).
- Get added to the official class WhatsApp/Telegram group same day. This is where most important info (schedule changes, assignment deadlines) flows.

**Ragging awareness (important, don't skip this)**
- Anti-ragging is taken seriously now, but informal "senior interactions" still happen in many colleges. Be polite and friendly with seniors, but if anything crosses into humiliation, forced tasks, or feels wrong — it's illegal. Note down details and call the national anti-ragging helpline (**1800-180-5522**) or inform the college anti-ragging cell.
- Most ragging today is social/psychological pressure rather than physical — good to know it exists and it's not "normal" or something to just endure.

**Mindset**
- First day is about orientation, not performance. No one expects them to "know" anything yet — including coding.

---

## First Week

- **Understand the syllabus and credit structure** — most CS programs start with Math (calculus/discrete math), Physics/Chemistry, a programming language (usually C or Python), and one humanities/soft-skill paper. Get a sense of what's graded via internals vs. end-sem exams.
- **Find 3–4 people to sit with regularly** — assignments, lab records, and "what did sir say" info travels through small peer groups. Isolation in week 1 makes everything harder later.
- **Locate seniors from CS/relevant clubs** — coding club, ACM/IEEE student chapter if it exists. Seniors are the fastest way to learn which professor is strict, which lab actually matters, and where old question papers are.
- **Don't sign up for everything on day one** — clubs, committees, and fests will all pitch hard in week one. Pick 1–2 things max initially.
- **Set up a proper coding environment** — a good code editor (VS Code), a Git/GitHub account, and basic comfort with the command line. This pays off from week 2 onward.

## First Few Months

### Academics
- **Attendance matters a lot** — most colleges have a hard 75% attendance cutoff to even sit for exams. Missing classes casually in month 1 because "it's just intro stuff" is a common regret.
- **Don't fall behind on math** — first-year math (calculus, linear algebra, discrete math) is foundational for later CS courses (algorithms, ML, graphics). Ask early if it feels shaky — not the week before exams.
- **Start coding beyond the syllabus** — college pace is slow. Solve problems on a platform (LeetCode/HackerRank/Codeforces) or build small personal projects alongside classwork. This matters a lot for internships later.
- **Understand the exam pattern** — internals (assignments, quizzes, mid-sems) usually carry real weight in Indian universities; don't dismiss them as "not important."

### Social / Mental Health
- It's normal to feel behind compared to peers who already know some coding — many students come in with prior exposure. Skill gaps in month 1 aren't a sign of not belonging.
- Ragging/hierarchy culture, hostel adjustment, homesickness, and comparison with "toppers" are common sources of first-semester stress — check in on this beyond just "how's the course going."
- If hosteling, cover basics: laundry, food, budgeting, staying in touch with home without being clingy, and roommate boundaries.

---

## Do's
- Ask questions in class/lab even if it feels "dumb" — professors remember engaged students.
- Type code themselves rather than copy-pasting from friends' lab records.
- Explore beyond the syllabus — GitHub, open source, a personal project — even something small.
- Keep a rough budget if handling their own money for the first time.

## Don'ts
- Don't skip labs — practical exposure early sets the foundation.
- Don't get pressured into ragging culture as "harmless tradition" — keep a clear line in mind.
- Don't wait till the end of semester to clear doubts — engineering math and CS fundamentals compound quickly.
- Don't overcommit to clubs/fests at the cost of grades in semester one — there's time to specialize later.
