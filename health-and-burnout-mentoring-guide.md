# Health & Avoiding Burnout — Mentoring Session
*A 20-minute discussion guide*

---

## Opening (2 min)
Start with a quick, honest question to get people thinking:

> "On a scale of 1–10, how sustainable does your current pace feel? Not how productive — how *sustainable*."

No need to go around the room and share numbers out loud. Just let it sit with them.

---

## Key Definitions (3 min)

Ground the conversation with shared definitions before going deeper — people often use these words loosely.

**Burnout**
A state of physical, emotional, and mental exhaustion caused by prolonged, unmanaged stress — usually from feeling overworked, undervalued, or unable to keep up with demands. The World Health Organization defines it as an occupational phenomenon with three markers: exhaustion, growing mental distance or cynicism toward one's work, and reduced effectiveness. It's not laziness, and it's not "just being tired" — it builds up gradually over weeks or months.

**Mental Peace**
A state of inner calm and stability where the mind isn't constantly reactive, anxious, or overstimulated. It doesn't mean the absence of problems — it means being able to face problems without your thoughts spiraling or your emotions taking over. It comes from things like clarity, boundaries, rest, and a sense of control over your day.

**Healthy Life**
A balance across physical, mental, and social wellbeing — not just "not being sick." It includes consistent sleep, movement, nutrition, meaningful relationships, and time for recovery. A healthy life is sustainable by design — it doesn't rely on willpower or short bursts of discipline.

**Work-Life Balance**
The ability to meet the demands of work without it consistently crowding out rest, relationships, health, and personal time. It's dynamic, not 50/50 every day — some weeks lean more toward work, but it shouldn't stay that way indefinitely.

**Stress vs. Burnout**
Stress is often short-term and tied to a specific event (a deadline, a hard conversation). Burnout is what happens when stress becomes chronic and unaddressed — it changes how someone feels about their work and themselves, not just how they feel in the moment.

**Talking point:** *"Ask them: which of these definitions surprised you, or didn't match what you expected?"*

---

## 1. Why This Conversation Matters (3 min)

- Health isn't separate from career success — it's the foundation of it. You can't perform, learn, or grow in a depleted state.
- Burnout doesn't look like collapse. It usually looks like slowly declining creativity, patience, and motivation — long before anyone "breaks down."
- Early-career years often set lifelong habits. The patterns you build now (or don't) tend to stick.

**Talking point:** *"The goal isn't to work less. It's to build a pace you can sustain for 30 years, not 3."*

---

## 2. The Four Pillars of Sustainable Health (7 min)

Go through these quickly — don't lecture, ask questions.

### 🛌 Sleep
- 7–8 hours isn't a luxury, it's a performance requirement.
- Ask: *"Who here treats sleep as the first thing to cut when busy?"*
- Reframe: poor sleep = worse decisions, worse mood, worse output — it costs more than it saves.

### 🏃 Movement
- Doesn't need to be a gym routine. Even a 20-min walk changes energy and focus.
- Ask: *"What's one form of movement you actually enjoy?"*

### 🍽️ Nutrition & Energy
- Skipping meals or living on caffeine creates energy crashes that feel like "laziness" but aren't.
- Small habit: eat something with protein in the first hour of the day.

### 🧠 Mental Recovery
- Constant stimulation (phone, notifications, multitasking) prevents real rest.
- Ask: *"Do you have any part of your day with zero inputs — no phone, no screen?"*

---

## 3. Recognizing Burnout Early (4 min)

Burnout isn't just "being tired." Share these signs:

| Early Signs | Later Signs |
|---|---|
| Trouble focusing | Cynicism / detachment |
| Irritability | Feeling ineffective despite effort |
| Dreading small tasks | Physical symptoms (headaches, insomnia) |
| Skipping things you used to enjoy | Emotional numbness |

**Key point:** *Burnout is a signal, not a personal failure.* It usually means the system (workload, boundaries, expectations) needs to change — not that the person needs to try harder.

---

## 4. Practical Habits That Actually Work (3 min)

Keep it simple — pick 1–2, not all:

- **Protect one boundary** (e.g., no emails after 8pm, or a hard stop time).
- **Single-task** for at least part of the day — multitasking increases stress without increasing output.
- **Schedule recovery like a meeting** — it won't happen if it's left to "whenever there's time."
- **Check in with yourself weekly**: energy, mood, motivation — catch dips early.

---

## Closing Discussion Question (1 min)

End with something they can actually act on:

> "What's one small, realistic change you could make this week — not a huge life overhaul, just one thing?"

Let a couple of people share if time allows. End on encouragement, not a to-do list.

---

## Optional Follow-Up
Consider checking in with mentees 2–3 weeks later to see how their chosen habit is going. Accountability (not pressure) helps things stick.
